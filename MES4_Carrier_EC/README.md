# MES4-Carrier-EC

Production carrier for one **MES4-MCU** M.2 module (STM32G0B1) on the **E-Crawler**. It drives four 24 V relay outputs and the winch direction relays, reads two Autonics sensors and a load cell, talks CAN, and powers the IMU, the screen and the door servo. The circuits come from the MES4-Flasher, with the relay channels per MES4-CIF §7.

Monkey Robotics · Rev A · KiCad 9 · Designed by Aaron Raj Bhatti

> **Work in progress:** see [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md) before changing anything. The schematic is generated by `generator/run.sh`. This board **deliberately differs from the MES4-CIF §2c GPIO table**: it has no relay coil feedback, so PA0 / PA1 drive the winch relays and PA2 / PA3 are the door servo UART (USART2).

## What it does

- **Four relay outputs** (switched 24 V, 2 A continuous each, CIF §7):
  - 1 safety light, 2 LED DRL, 3 battery stopper (1.5 A solenoid, intermittent), 4 spare;
  - AO3400A coil driver with a 100k gate pull-down, so every relay is OFF during reset and boot;
  - no coil readback (its pins drive the winch and the servo UART): the INA236 does the diagnosis;
  - an INA236 per channel measuring the load current and the 24 V rail;
  - a PTC per output, an SS34 load flyback and an "output on" LED.
- **Two Autonics sensor inputs** for 3-wire PNP 24 V sensors. Each has a PC817 opto input (as on the MES4-Flasher), a 100 mA supply PTC and a 100 nF / 50 V cap at the terminal.
- **Load-cell input**: the MES4-Flasher circuit unchanged. ADS1232 24-bit ADC, ratiometric 5 V excitation, RC filters and BAV199 clamps.
- **CAN** on a male DB9 to the IPC (1 GND, 3 CAN_H, 5 CAN_L) and a 1×4 RJ45 jack without magnetics (1 CAN_H, 2 CAN_L, 3 GND on every port). All five ports are wired in parallel on one bus, with an H24VNT2B ESD clamp at each connector.
- **ST-Link V3MINIE** on the STDC14 connector (SWD, NRST, VCP), plus a reset button.
- **24 V input on an XT30** (the MES4-Flasher's right-angle part) with the noise filtering described below.
- **IMU power, 9 V**: the MES3.1 carrier's "IMU Power Supply @ 9V" (SS12D10G5 switch, 2-pin terminal) on a URB2409S-6WR3: the MES3.1's 10 W URB2409S-10WR3 isn't in JLC's assembly library.
- **Screen power on USB-C**: 5 V / 3 A for the ARZOPA Z1C portable monitor, on the MES4-Flasher's USB-C receptacle.
- **Door servo (Feetech, via the FE-URT-2 board)**: 24 V supply + UART on USART2, the same two terminals as on the MES2 Harvester.
- **Winch control**: the MES2_Extension's two IM03GR direction relays (CLK / ACLK) for the 48 V winch controller, with a hardware interlock.

## GPIO budget

All 14 module GPIOs are used. The pins are checked against the MES4-MCU netlist. This differs from the MES4-CIF §2c table on one point: the relay coil feedback (PA0–PA3 in the CIF) is left out, which frees PA0 / PA1 for the winch relays and PA2 / PA3 (USART2) for the door servo. Without it, an open coil or a dead driver shows up only as "relay ON, no current" on the INA236, the same as a contact that doesn't close.

| Module pin (finger) | Use |
|---|---|
| PA8 / PC6 / PB3 / PA7 (31 / 29 / 61 / 35) | Relay 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 drive (CIF §2c) |
| PA0 (49) / PA1 (47) | Winch CLK / ACLK relay drive (K5 / K6) |
| PA2 (45) / PA3 (43) | Door servo UART TX / RX (USART2) → J17 |
| PA15 (63) | INA236 ALERT (all four wire-OR'd, 10k pull-up) |
| PA4 / PA5 / PA6 (41 / 39 / 37) | ADS1232 DRDY/DOUT, SCLK, PDWN |
| PB6 / PC15 (59 / 51) | Sensor 1 / 2: HIGH = sensor output on |
| I²C1 SDA / SCL (53 / 55) | Four INA236s (2.2k pull-ups) |

| I²C device | Address | Measures / does |
|---|---|---|
| U2 / U3 / U4 / U5 INA236 | 0x40 / 0x41 / 0x42 / 0x43 (A0 = GND / VS / SDA / SCL) | Relay 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 load current and the 24 V rail (read on IN−) |

Firmware notes:
- **EXTI15:** PC15 (sensor 2) and PA15 (ALERT) share EXTI line 15. Give the interrupt to PC15 and poll ALERT; the INA236 latches it (MASK/ENABLE 0x8001), so no fault is missed.
- **PB6:** the ST bootloader enables a pull-up on PB6 on a blank chip only. With the 10k pull-down it reads LOW, which is harmless for an input.
- **Relay diagnosis:** INA236 only (no coil feedback). Read ≥ 30 ms after switching: OFF with current > 3 mA = welded contact or shorted driver; ON with no current = the relay didn't close (coil, driver, contact, PTC, fuse or 24 V). The 6.7 mA "output on" LED makes a closed contact visible with no load.
- **USART2:** the ST bootloader also probes PA2 / PA3 on a blank chip, so flash over SWD first (the normal flow).
- **Winch:** drive only one of PA0 / PA1 at a time. The hardware interlock opens both outputs if both are on, but don't rely on it as the only guard.

## Power

**24 V input chain:** J5 XT30 → F1 fuse → Q1 P-MOSFET reverse-polarity block → D9 SMBJ30A TVS + C1 100 µF bulk + C2 / C3 ceramics → **+24V** (relay contacts, INA236 VBUS, sensor supplies).

**Logic supply:** +24V → L1 + C4 / C5 pi filter → U1 URB2405S-6WR3 (isolated, 5 V 1.2 A) → **+5V_24** (relay coils) → F2 1.1 A PTC + FB1 + C8 / C9 pi filter → **+5V** (module fingers 20 / 22, ADS1232 AVDD).

Noise filtering, from the cable inwards:
1. D9 SMBJ30A clamps surges and inductive spikes on the 24 V bus.
2. C1 electrolytic damps the cable inductance, so hot-plugging doesn't ring above 24 V.
3. C2 / C3 ceramics: low impedance for high frequencies at the input.
4. The L1 pi filter feeds only the DC/DC. The logic supply is decoupled from the bus, and the converter's switching noise stays off it.
5. One ground: the 24 V return and the logic ground are one net, GND, with a solid plane on In1 (the old GND_24V / NT1 split is gone). The load returns (relay outputs J6–J9, servo J15) run east to J5, away from the load cell and the module.
6. Another pi filter (FB1) on the module 5 V keeps relay-coil switching off the module and the load-cell excitation. FB2 filters the ADS1232 AVDD again.
7. Flyback diodes sit at every relay coil (1N4148W) and every output (SS34).

The Flasher blocks a reversed lead with an SS56 Schottky. At this board's current (up to ~8 A with all four outputs loaded) that diode would dissipate 3–4 W, so a P-MOSFET does the job here.

YLPTEC's EMC circuit for the URB2405S adds a 470 µH common-mode choke and 1 nF Y-capacitors. They are not fitted: the board has one ground, so the converter's isolation isn't used, and a differential pi filter (L1) does the filtering instead. YLPTEC also lists hot-plug as unsupported for this converter. L1 slows the inrush into C5, and Q1, D9 and C1 absorb the XT30 plug-in.

## CAN

The module's transceiver (and its 120 Ω terminator behind JP1) is on the MES4-MCU. The carrier only brings CANH / CANL (fingers 1 / 3) out to five ports in parallel:

| Port | Pinout | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| J3 DB9 male, to the IPC | 1 GND, 3 CAN_H, 5 CAN_L | Matches the IPC's port. The IPC side is male too, so use a straight-through **female–female** DB9 cable (1–1, 3–3, 5–5). This is **not** the CiA 303-1 DB9 pinout (2 CAN_L, 7 CAN_H, 3 GND), so a standard CAN DB9 cable or adapter won't match. |
| J4 RJ45 ×4 | 1 CAN_H (white-orange), 2 CAN_L (orange), 3 GND (white-green) | T568B colours, CiA 303-1 pinout, same as the MES4-Flasher RJ45. CAN_H / CAN_L share the orange twisted pair. |

- **Keep the bus a line.** Run it IPC → DB9 → this board → one RJ45 → next node, and so on. Any port used as a branch is a stub: keep each stub ≤ 0.3 m at 500 kbit/s (CIF §5).
- **Exactly two 120 Ω terminators**, at the two physical ends of the bus. Bridge JP1 on the module only if this board is an end.
- **No 24 V in any CAN cable or connector** (CIF rule 9). No series resistors on CAN_H / CAN_L (CIF rule 8).
- **ESD:** D6 at the DB9 and D7 at the RJ45 bank (H24VNT2B). The DB9 shell and the RJ45 shield tabs go to GND.

## IMU and screen power (page 9)

Both converters run from their own pi filter: L2 (the same 10 µH part as L1) with C26 / C27 off +24V. This keeps the screen's 20 W converter off the logic supply's L1 filter. Their input and output sides share the one GND, as U1's do.

**IMU, 9 V.** The MES3.1-Carrier-LH "IMU Power Supply @ 9V" with the same parts: U9 YLPTEC URB2409S-6WR3 (9 V, 0.67 A, 6 W isolated; the IMU draws ≈ 45 mA) with C28 47 µF / 100 V in and C29 22 µF / 16 V out, the MES3.1's values. It is U1's family, footprint and pinout; the MES3.1 used the 10 W URB2409S-10WR3, which JLC can't assemble. SW2 (SS12D10G5) switches it to J14. LED8 (green, with R35 2.2k at 3.2 mA) sits after SW2, so it lights only when the IMU terminal is powered.

**Screen, USB-C 5 V / 3 A.** The ARZOPA Z1C's label reads "5V/9V/12V/15V/20VDC 3A MAX", so it's a USB-PD sink. Without a PD controller a Type-C source can only offer 5 V, which gives 15 W. Its sibling, the Z1RC, draws about 9 W (≈ 1.8 A at 5 V), so 5 V / 3 A should run it. **Check full brightness on the bench.** If it needs 9 V or more, this port needs a USB-PD source controller instead.

The chain is:
- U10 YLPTEC URB2405YMD-20WR3: 5 V 4 A, 20 W isolated, 1″ × 1″, CTRL and Trim open; C38 100 µF / 16 V output bulk;
- C33 / C34 22 µF;
- F11 3 A PTC (the relay outputs' part);
- D22 SMF6.0A;
- J16, the MES4-Flasher's USB-C power-only receptacle. It is rated 5 V 3 A: 3 A on VBUS, 1.25 A per GND pin.

R40 / R41 (10k Rp to 5 V on CC2 / CC1) advertise Type-C current 3.0 A. The shell goes to GND through R42 1 MΩ ∥ C37 2.2 µF, as on the Flasher. The port carries power only: video goes over the monitor's own HDMI / USB-C cable from the IPC.

### Door servo (Feetech, via the FE-URT-2)

The MES2 Harvester drives its servo through Feetech's FE-URT-2 board, which turns a normal UART into the half-duplex servo bus and passes the supply through to the servos. This board copies its two terminals, so the Harvester's cables fit:
- **J15 SERVO 24V**: pin 1 = +24 V, pin 2 = GND, like every other power terminal on this board. The Harvester's CN5 has them the other way round, so re-pin that cable. +24 V comes through F9, a 3 A PTC (the relay outputs' part), plus C31 100 nF and C32 47 µF.
- **J17 SERVO UART** (CN13 on the Harvester): GND, +5 V, RX, TX.

The UART uses module **USART2**: PA2 TX (finger 45) → R39 100 Ω → pin 4, and pin 3 → R38 1 kΩ → PA3 RX (finger 43, 5 V-tolerant). There are H5VSD3B ESD clamps (D23 / D24) at the connector, per CIF rule 8. R36 10k holds RX idle-HIGH when the FE-URT-2 is unplugged, and R37 10k holds TX idle-HIGH while PA2 floats during MCU reset and boot, so the FE-URT-2 never sees a floating input. R38 is 1 kΩ (not 100 Ω) so that if the module is off while the FE-URT-2 is powered, its TX can push at most a few mA into PA3 (MES4-CIF rule 6); that's still fast enough for 1 Mbit/s.

**Set the FE-URT-2's level switch to 3.3 V, not 5 V.** The switch only sets the UART signal levels on J17 pins 3 / 4. The servo 24 V (J15) and the FE-URT-2's own 5 V (J17 pin 2) don't change. At 5 V, the module's 3.3 V TX may not reliably read as HIGH, the FE-URT-2's TX back-feeds the module 3V3 through R36, and at power-up (FE-URT-2 alive before the module) it exceeds PA3's VDD + 4 V limit. After setting it, read a servo's ID or position once on the bench to confirm the servo bus works at 3.3 V.

The FE-URT-2's 5 V comes from the screen converter through F10 (1.1 A PTC). A short on the servo side then can't pull down the module's supply.

## Winch control (page 10)

These are the MES2_Extension's "WINCH CONTROL" relays: two TE **IM03GR** (C2153100, 1,497 in stock), 5 V coil (178 Ω, 28 mA) from +5V_24, DPDT 2 A / 220 VDC. They switch the 48 V winch controller's direction inputs to J18 pin 2 (COM): pin 1 = CLK, pin 3 = ACLK. They carry signal current only, not the winch motor current (60 W DC switching limit).

| Direction | Module pin | Relay | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLK | PA0 (finger 49) | K5 | Q6 AO3400A, R43 100 Ω gate, R44 100k pull-down |
| ACLK | PA1 (finger 47) | K6 | Q7 AO3400A, R45 100 Ω gate, R46 100k pull-down |

- **Contacts:** from TE's terminal assignment: coil 1 (+) / 8 (−); pole 1 is 3 COM, 2 NC, 4 NO; pole 2 is 6 COM, 7 NC, 5 NO. Pole 1 (3 → 4) is the Extension's switching contact.
- **Drivers:** the Extension drove the coils with 2SC2884 transistors and no pull-down. Here they follow the relay channels (CIF §7a), so both relays stay OFF while PA0 / PA1 float during reset and boot. D25 / D26 are the coil flybacks.
- **Hardware interlock (new, using the spare pole 2):** each direction's common is fed through the other relay's resting contact (NC2). CLK can close only while K6 is at rest, and ACLK only while K5 is at rest. If both are energised, both outputs stay open.
- **Isolation from the 48 V winch system:** J18 and the contact-side nets (WINCH_CLK / COM / ACLK, ILK_CLK / ILK_ACLK) connect only to relay contacts, never to carrier GND or a supply. The IM03GR's coil-to-contact insulation (TE IM series, up to 2500 Vrms) is the barrier. PCB: these nets are in net class `Winch_48V`; keep ≥ 2 mm from all other copper and no GND pour under J18 or the contact pins. Don't add a TVS or ESD diode to GND on J18: it would bridge the isolation.

## Limits

- **No hot-plug** (CIF rule 5). There is no module power switch: unplug the XT30 before inserting or removing a module.
- **J12 / J13 are 5 V load-cell terminals** of the same push-in type as the 24 V outputs. A 24 V wire there damages the module and the ADC. Countermeasure (no keyed connector, to keep one terminal type): the silkscreen names them "5V LOAD CELL", one label across both, and they sit away from the 24 V terminals.
- **J4 RJ45 ports are CAN, not Ethernet.** An Ethernet cable or passive PoE on them damages the bus: a "CANBUS" knockout on the silkscreen beside the bank; label the panel the same.
- **3 A PTCs derate** to about 2.2 A hold at 60 °C (F3–F6, F9, F11): still above the 2 A relay rating, but check against the enclosure temperature.
- **Sensor inputs are for PNP (sourcing) 24 V sensors.** NPN types need a different circuit.
- **Never run CAN in a cable or connector with 24 V** (CIF rule 9).
- **The screen port's VBUS is always on** (no cold-socket switch): plug in only the monitor or another sink, never a charger or a laptop.
- **FE-URT-2 at 3.3 V levels only** (its level switch): at 5 V it overdrives PA3 when the module is unpowered. See Door servo.
- **IMU: MicroStrain 3DM-CV7-AHRS on the C-Series Connectivity Board** (6212-3009 USB development kit). The CV7 takes 3.2–5.2 V and 0.23 W; the board's Vaux input takes 5.2–28 V through its own TPS54260 buck. Wire J14's 9 V to **Vaux** (micro-DB9 pin 6), **never Vpri** (pin 3, straight to the CV7: 9 V there destroys it). Use Vaux or Vpri, not both: switch SW2 off while the USB dev cable (which puts the PC's 5 V on Vpri) is plugged in. Simulated draw ≈ 45 mA at 9 V, a tenth of SW2's 0.5 A rating (sim IMU1).
- **Servo supply is the raw 24 V bus** (as on the Harvester): only for 24 V servo types. F9 trips at 5 A.
- **Input fuse margin:** a realistic total of 6–8 A is fine. Every load at its limit at once (4 × 2 A relays + 3 A servo + IMU + screen ≈ 13 A) exceeds F1's 75 % derating (11 A).
