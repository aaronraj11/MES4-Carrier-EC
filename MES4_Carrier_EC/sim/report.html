3 · Open items for the reviewer

  • F11 (screen PTC): keep 3 A hold, or fit 4 A now? It depends on the ARZOPA Z1C's real current (AUX1, L7).
  • Supply limits: the pack's full-charge voltage (7S Li-ion 29.4 V or 8S LiFePO4 29.2 V) against the AB20 variant (A/B/C rated 30 V, D rated 60 V).
  • Datasheet checks: YLPTEC converters at 40 V for about 1 ms (PWR2); Q1 AGM60P90D IDM against the 100 A plug-in inrush; F1 melting I²t against 0.95 A²s (PWR1).
  • Hold-up: 4.0 ms on total power loss (PWR6). Enough for glitches; add bulk on +24V only if a clean shutdown is wanted.

5 · Power · sheet Power (page 4)

Supply into J5 (your system): 48 V main battery → Mean Well DDR-480C-24 (set to 24 V; adjustable 24–28 V; OVP 28.8–35 V; 4 kV isolated; 20 A) → AB20 line 1. 24 V 12 Ah Li-ion pack → XY-CD63L discharge monitor → AB20 line 2. The AB20 is a diode-OR ("zero-delay", anti-backflow): the higher input feeds the carrier. Model: source_ab20.inc.

Parts: J5 XT30, F1 15 A, Q1 AGM60P90D + D8/R6, D9 SMBJ30A, C1–C3 · L1 + C4/C5 → U1 URB2405S-6WR3 (+24V_DC) · L2 + C26–C30 → U9 / U10 (AuxPower) · F9 + C31/C32 (servo). Shared model: common.inc.

PWR1 · hot-plug (pwr1_hotplug.net)

The XT30 closes on the AB20 output with the pack fully charged (29.4 V, above the DDR's 24 V, so the pack feeds the carrier), through a 2 m cable (2 µH, 33 mΩ). +24V rises cleanly to 29.1 V, with no overshoot on any branch, so D9 never conducts. Charging about 450 µF pulls a current pulse peaking at 100 A and lasting about 0.1 ms (F1 I²t 0.95 A²s), so the XT30 will spark. L1 and L2 saturate briefly (31 / 46 A in this linear model). That's harmless; confirm Q1's pulsed drain current and F1's melting I²t on their datasheets.

PWR2 · surge at D9's rating (pwr2_tvs_pulse.net)

This is the design-limit case: both AB20 inputs are gone (bus held at 29.2 V by a weak source) and a 12.4 A, 10/1000 µs pulse arrives on the cable. With the AB20's diodes blocking any return path, this is also what servo back-EMF would face. The bulk capacitors take most of it: the bus peaks at 40.0 V and D9 takes 4.4 A and 0.31 J of its ≈ 0.87 J rating. That leaves 10 V of margin to the INA236. U1, U9 and U10 see 40 V for about 1 ms, above their 36 V operating range, so check YLPTEC's input surge rating (similar Mornsun URB parts allow 50 V for 1 s).

PWR3 · IEC 61000-4-5 surge (pwr3_iec_surge.net) · not applicable

Not applicable to this system: the carrier's 24 V comes from the isolated DDR-480C-24 and an internal pack through the AB20, on short wiring inside the robot. The run is kept for reference, in case the bus ever gets an external charger port or a long harness.

0.5 kV line-to-line, 1.2/50 µs, 2 Ω, through an 18 µF coupling capacitor, with the battery behind a 1.5 mH decoupling inductor. D9 conducts 27 A and the bus reaches 62.9 V, above the INA236's 50 V and the 50 V ceramics. +24V_AUX stays at 39.7 V behind L2. This surge is normally required only for long or external DC power cables. If the bus ever goes off the robot (a charger port, a long harness), fit an SMCJ30A (1500 W, same standoff, Vc 48.4 V at 31 A, about 47 V here).

PWR4 · reverse battery (pwr4_reverse.net)

The battery is connected as −29.2 V. Q1's body diode is reverse-biased and its gate stays off, so +24V stays between −4 mV and +2 mV and only 0.12 mA of leakage flows. D9 sits after Q1, so it never sees the reverse voltage. Pass

PWR6 · line changeover and hold-up (pwr6_changeover.net)

The DDR on line 1 carries the load at 24 V (the carrier sees 23.6 V after the AB20 diode), with the pack part-discharged at 23.0 V. At 2 ms the DDR stops (48 V battery below its 33 V UVLO, or an OVP trip). The pack takes over through the AB20 with no gap: +24V simply steps to 22.5 V. At 12 ms the XY-CD63L cuts the pack as well. With both sources gone, the carrier keeps running on its own bulk capacitance for 4.0 ms (screen at full load) before U1's input falls to 9 V and the module loses 5 V. That's enough to ride through glitches, not enough to shut down cleanly. If you ever want a clean shutdown on total power loss, add bulk capacitance on +24V (2,200 µF would give about 20 ms). Also, the DDR alone can never put more than 35 V on the bus (its OVP), which is inside the converters' 36 V and the INA236's 50 V.

PWR5 · 5 V rails (pwr5_5v_rail.net, sheets Power + Main)

Parts: U1 output → C6/C7 (+5V_24) → F2 1.1 A PTC → FB1 → C8/C9 → module fingers 20/22 (module 10 µF + SMF6.0A); relay K1–K4 and winch K5 coils on +5V_24; load cell excitation via FB2.

The module steps from 40 mA to 240 mA at 2 ms, then four relay coils and one winch coil switch on together at 5 ms (0.27 A after 5 ms, rising to 0.31 A). The module's +5V stays between 4.91 V and 4.99 V, inside its 4.75–5.5 V window, and +5V_24 never drops below 4.95 V.

6 · Relays · sheets Relays 1-2 / Relays 3-4 (pages 5–6)

Parts per channel: GPIO → R9 100 Ω (R10 100k pull-down) → Q2 AO3400A → K1 SRD-5VDC-SL-A coil, D10 1N4148W flyback; contact → F3 3 A PTC → J6, D12 SS34 load flyback, LED4 + R14; R8 30 mΩ shunt → U2 INA236. Same for channels 2–4.

RLY1 · coil driver (rly1_coil_driver.net)

3.3 V GPIO on at 0.2 ms, off at 10.2 ms. The gate rises in 0.28 µs, and the coil draws 69 mA. At turn-off, D10 clamps the drain at 5.8 V (the AO3400A is rated 30 V). The coil current takes 3.9 ms to fall to 10 mA, which delays the relay's release a little. That's normal with a plain diode flyback and fine for lamps and the solenoid.

RLY2 · solenoid switched off (rly2_solenoid_off.net, channel 3 battery stopper)

A 24 V, 1.5 A solenoid (16 Ω, 50 mH assumed) is on when K3 opens at 5 ms. D16 SS34 clamps the output at −0.36 V and carries 1.49 A (rated 3 A). The current decays to 10 % in 6.9 ms. The INA236's IN− sits on the supply side of the contact and stays at 23.96 V, so the flyback never reaches its −0.3 V input limit.

7 · Sensors · sheet Sensors (page 7)

Parts: J10 → F7 100 mA PTC → sensor; sensor output → R24 10k → U6 PC817 (D18 reverse clamp) → PB6 with R25 10k pull-down, collector on +3V3_MOD. Sensor 2 (PC15) is identical.

SNS1 · PC817 input (sns1_pc817.net)

This is the weak case: the sensor output is only 20 V (low battery) and the PC817 has its minimum CTR of 50 %. The LED draws 1.84 mA. PB6 goes HIGH to 3.17 V, well above the STM32's 2.31 V threshold, with about 3× margin on CTR. Rise is 4 µs and fall is 34 µs, much faster than any proximity sensor switches.

8 · Load cell · sheet LoadCell (page 8)

Parts: J12 SIG → R28/R29 100 Ω → C17 100 nF differential + C16/C18 10 nF to GND → D20/D21 BAV199 clamps → U8 ADS1232 (gain 128, 10 SPS). Source: two SOPLF-102 cells in parallel (700 Ω each → 350 Ω).

LC1 · input filter (lc1_input_filter.net, AC)

The differential signal is flat in the passband (−0.001 dB at 50 Hz) and rolls off at −3 dB at 2.76 kHz, including the cells' 350 Ω source. Common-mode noise converts to a differential error only through component mismatch. With C18 10 % low, that's −56 dB at 1 kHz, worst ≈ −47 dB around 20 kHz and −71 dB at 1 MHz. The ADS1232's 10 SPS digital filter removes what's left at those frequencies. The C0G capacitors keep the mismatch small and stable.

9 · Aux power · sheet AuxPower (page 9)

Parts: L2 + C26/C27/C28/C30 → U9 URB2409S-10WR3 (IMU 9 V) and U10 URB2405LD-20WR3 (screen 5 V) → C33/C34 → F11 3 A PTC → J16 USB-C · F9 3 A PTC + C31/C32 → J15 servo 24 V · servo UART J17: R39 100 Ω / R38 1k series, D23/D24 ESD, R36/R37 10k pull-ups.

AUX1 · U10 start-up (aux1_u10_startup.net)

With the bus up, U10 soft-starts its 5 V output in 2 ms (fast-case assumption) into C33/C34, F11 and the screen, which pulls the full 3 A. L2 carries 0.79 A in total (U9 with the IMU + U10), far below its saturation current, and +24V_AUX dips only to 23.5 V. F11 peaks at 3.25 A and sits at 3.0 A, exactly its 23 °C hold current. In a warm enclosure (≈ 2.2 A hold at 60 °C) it would trip if the screen really draws 3 A.

AUX2 · servo stall step (aux2_servo_step.net)

The servo draws 3 A for 5 ms (stall), supplied through the AB20 from the DDR at 24 V over a 2 m cable. +24V_SRV dips to 23.19 V and the main +24V to 23.32 V. The relays, INA236s and +24V_AUX (23.27 V) barely notice.

AUX3 · servo UART (aux3_servo_uart.net)

1 Mbit/s (Feetech default) over 0.3 m, with the FE-URT-2 at 3.3 V levels. TX edges at the FE-URT-2 rise in 4 ns. RX edges at PA3 rise in 8.8 ns through the 1 kΩ R38: both under 1 % of the 1 µs bit. PA3's LOW level is 0.31 V (1k against the 10k pull-up), under the STM32's 0.99 V threshold.

10 · IMU · sheet AuxPower (page 9) → J14 → MicroStrain dev kit

Parts: U9 URB2409S-10WR3 9 V → C29 → SW2 SS12D10G5 → LED8 / R35 → J14 → 1 m cable → C-Series Connectivity Board (part of the 6212-3009 USB development kit) Vaux input → its TPS54260 buck → 5 V → 3DM-CV7-AHRS (3.2–5.2 V, 0.23 W, per HBK's product page and the board's Interface Control Document).

IMU1 · switching the IMU on (imu1_devkit_switch_on.net)

SW2 closes with U9's 9 V already up. The 1 m cable and the board's ceramic input capacitors (2 × 2.2 µF + 0.1 µF) ring: Vaux overshoots to 14.3 V, inside the board's 5.2–28 V Vaux range. The inrush through SW2 peaks at 9.1 A for a few microseconds (0.3 mJ of capacitor charge). Then the buck soft-starts, and the steady draw is 44 mA at J14, 48 mA through SW2 with LED8. That's about a tenth of SW2's 0.5 A rating, so review item L8's SW2 check is closed. U9 (1.11 A) runs at about 4 % load.

Wiring rules from the dev kit's Interface Control Document, not things a simulation can show:

  • Feed J14's 9 V into the board's Vaux input (micro-DB9 pin 6, "+4 V to +35 VDC" in the pin table, 5.2–28 V in the schematic notes). Never into Vpri (pin 3, 3.2–5.2 V): Vpri goes straight to the CV7, and 9 V there would destroy it.
  • Vaux or Vpri, never both. The USB dev cable (micro-DB9 to USB) powers Vpri from the PC's 5 V. When the USB cable is plugged in for configuration, switch SW2 off.
  • J14 pin 2 is the carrier's logic GND. Over USB the IMU also connects to the PC's ground: normal, but keep the IMU's USB and power cables together.

11 · Winch · sheet Winch (page 10)

Parts: PA0 → R43 100 Ω (R44 100k) → Q6 AO3400A → K5 IM03GR coil (178 Ω), D25 1N4148W flyback. K6 / PA1 is identical. The contact side (J18) is isolated and passive.

WCH1 · coil driver (wch1_coil_driver.net)

The coil draws 28 mA. At turn-off, D25 clamps the drain at 5.75 V, and the current falls to 3 mA in 0.87 ms, so release is quick.

12 · Main · sheet Main (page 2), devices on Relays (pages 5–6)

Parts: R3/R4 2.2k I²C1 pull-ups and R5 10k ALERT pull-up to +3V3_MOD; four INA236 (U2–U5) and the STM32 on the bus.

MAIN1 · I²C and ALERT (main1_i2c.net)

400 kHz with an estimated 60 pF of bus capacitance. SCL rises (30–70 %) in 112 ns, against the 300 ns fast-mode limit, and the LOW level is 0.09 V. ALERT (10k, 40 pF) rises in 0.34 µs, which is plenty for an interrupt or polled line.

14 · Models and assumptions

  • Values come from the schematic netlist. Ceramic 10 µF / 50 V parts are modelled at ≈ 4 µF at 24–30 V, and 22 µF / 25 V at ≈ 15–18 µF at 5 V (DC bias).
  • Electrolytic ESR: 100 µF / 63 V 0.25 Ω, 47 µF / 100 V 0.6 Ω (datasheet-class, not measured).
  • Supply: DDR-480C-24 at 24 V (10 mΩ); pack 29.4 V full (7S Li-ion; 8S LiFePO4 would be 29.2 V), 60 mΩ assumed; XY-CD63L switch 5 mΩ assumed; AB20 Schottky diodes ≈ 0.45 V at 10 A (assumed; Autoro gives no drop figure). Cable from the AB20 to the carrier 2 m (2 µH, 33 mΩ).
  • SMBJ30A: Zener model fitted to Vc 48.4 V at 12.4 A. AO3400A: generic VDMOS (Vth 1.05 V). 1N4148W and SS34: generic diode models. PC817: behavioural, CTR 50 %, 20 µs pole.
  • Converters are constant-power loads above 9 V (U1 3.75 W, U9 0.5 W for the CV7 dev kit, U10 17 W), with 5 ms soft start (U10's output ramp 2 ms in AUX1). Screen input 100 µF, drawing 3 A.
  • Assumed inductances (not on the datasheets): SRD coil 0.2 H, IM03GR 0.1 H, battery-stopper solenoid 50 mH. Inductors are linear (no saturation).
  • Bus capacitance: I²C 60 pF, ALERT 40 pF, PA3 7 pF. UART cable 0.3 m (100 Ω, 1.5 ns).
